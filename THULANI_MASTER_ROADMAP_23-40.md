## PART 3 — FINANCIAL & INVESTMENT MODEL

**This is a framework, not personalized financial or legal advice — you are not obligated to follow allocation percentages exactly, and you should validate the tax and investment-product specifics with a licensed South African financial advisor and/or tax practitioner before committing large sums, since TFSA limits, unit trust fee structures, and property finance terms change over time.**

### 3.1 Core Allocation Blueprint (South African instruments)

Once emergency fund (3–6 months expenses) is secured, route new savings in this priority order:

1. **Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA)** — max the annual contribution limit every year without fail (check SARS's current annual and lifetime limits each tax year, they are indexed and change). This is your highest-priority vehicle because growth and withdrawals are both tax-free for life. Prioritize a low-cost equity index tracker inside the TFSA (e.g., an all-share or S&P 500 feeder tracker) over active unit trusts — fees compound against you over 17 years.
2. **Retirement Annuity (RA)** — contribute enough to capture the full tax deduction (currently up to 27.5% of taxable income, capped annually — verify the current cap each year) without over-allocating, since RA funds are illiquid until retirement age and won't help fund a house or education costs in your 30s.
3. **Discretionary unit trust / ETF portfolio (outside the TFSA)** — this is your main growth engine once the TFSA is maxed. Low-cost, broad-market ETFs (local + offshore) held for 15+ years, not actively traded.
4. **Offshore/Rand-hedge exposure** — a meaningful portion (guideline: 25–40% of your discretionary portfolio by your 30s) should sit outside Rand-denominated assets, given long-run currency depreciation risk. Offshore-feeder unit trusts or direct offshore brokerage access both work.
5. **Property** — see 3.3 below, funded from a dedicated deposit sub-account, never raided for other goals.
6. **LaughTale reinvestment** — profit not needed for personal runway gets reinvested into the business (tooling, hiring, marketing) rather than pulled out as a distribution, especially in Phases I–II while the business is still compounding.

### 3.2 The Six Education Funds — Exact Mechanics

You need 6 separate, ring-fenced accounts: 1 for your sister, 5 for future children. Do not commingle them — six sub-accounts (most SA unit trust platforms allow multiple named accounts under one umbrella) or six separate TFSA-adjacent investment accounts.

**Target per fund:** a 4-year South African tertiary education (tuition + residence + living costs) at a public university currently runs roughly R150,000–R250,000/year in today's money; assume R200,000/year as a working average, R800,000 total per child in today's terms. Private/specialized programs run higher — build in a buffer.

**Inflation adjustment is critical:** education cost inflation in South Africa has historically outpaced general CPI. Model each fund's target using **8–9% annual education-cost inflation**, not general inflation, when calculating the future rand value needed.

### 3.3 Real Estate Strategy — The 6+ Bedroom Family Home

**Do not attempt to buy the final family home first.** Two-stage strategy:

**Stage 1 (Age 29–30): Starter/stepping-stone property.** A smaller, lower-cost property (freehold or sectional title) bought with a 10–20% deposit and a standard 20-year bond. Purpose: build equity, build a repayment track record, and stop paying rent into someone else's asset. This is not the forever-home — it may even be rented out later as an additional income stream once you upgrade, which also strengthens your net worth line.

**Stage 2 (Age 32–35): The family home.** Two realistic paths, decide by age 31 which one you're executing:
- **Path A — Buy and renovate/extend:** Purchase an existing large plot/home in a growth-area suburb and extend to 6+ bedrooms. Faster, but land + renovation costs can exceed new-build costs in desirable areas.
- **Path B — Build from plot:** Purchase land, then build to spec with 5 private children's bedrooms plus master and guest/study space designed from the start. Slower (18–30 months build time) but gives exact control over layout — the more reliable path for hitting "every child has their own room" exactly as specified rather than retrofitting.

**Funding mechanics:** target a 20–30% deposit on Stage 2 funded primarily from Stage 1 property equity growth plus LaughTale distributions (not from the education funds or TFSA — those are firewalled). Bond the remainder over 20 years, sized so the repayment does not exceed roughly 25–30% of combined household net income at the time of purchase — this is the standard affordability discipline that keeps the education funds and investment contributions from being sacrificed to service the house.

- [ ] Stage 2 land/property secured by age 32

## PART 4 — DAILY & WEEKLY DISCIPLINE PROTOCOL

### 4.1 Design Principles (built around executive function, not against it)

- **Same skeleton every weekday.** The blocks below repeat identically Monday–Friday. Novelty in structure is where plans collapse — the content inside each block changes, the block itself never does.
- **Single-task blocks, hard stops.** No block mixes cert study and LaughTale work. Context-switching cost is real; the schedule is built to minimize it, not to maximize hours crammed in.
- **Written, not remembered.** Every day starts with a physical or digital checklist review, not a mental one. If it's not written down, it does not count as planned.
- **Explicit transition buffers.** 10–15 minutes between blocks are scheduled on purpose, not left implicit — this is where the day actually falls apart if it's skipped.
- **Recovery is scheduled, not earned.** Rest blocks are in the plan by default, not granted as a reward for finishing everything else.

### 4.4 Contingency Protocols — Burnout, Setbacks, Market Shifts

**Rule 1 — The 80% floor.** On any day where full execution isn't possible, the floor is: physical conditioning happens, and one single-task block (study OR LaughTale, not both) happens. The day is never a total zero. A zero day is the only real failure mode — a reduced day is not a failure.

**Rule 2 — Burnout triggers a scoped pause, not an open-ended one.** If burnout symptoms appear (persistent exhaustion, dread toward blocks that were previously fine, declining output quality despite maintained hours), you get a **defined 5–7 day reduced-load week** — conditioning and full-time job only, cert study and LaughTale paused — followed by a mandatory return to the full schedule. Undefined breaks are how 17-year plans die; defined ones protect the plan.

**Rule 3 — Missed certification date is a reschedule, not a crisis.** Move the exam date, keep the study block running. The cost of a missed exam date is small. The cost of abandoning the study habit while "regrouping" is large — never let the two get conflated.

**Rule 4 — Market/role shift response.** If the OT/cybersecurity job market shifts (redundancy, sector downturn, role elimination), the response sequence is: (1) activate the emergency fund, never the education funds or TFSA, (2) accelerate LaughTale client acquisition immediately as bridge income, (3) use the certification stack as-is to re-enter the market — the credentials exist specifically so you are not dependent on any single employer's continued goodwill.

**Rule 5 — Quarterly review is mandatory and written.** Every quarter: update the net worth spreadsheet, check every checkbox in this document against actual evidence, and write two sentences on what didn't work and what you're changing. This document is only a "bible" if it's actually revised against reality — treat it as version-controlled, not sacred and static.
